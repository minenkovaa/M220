    Section Module 1: Vectors

    Subsection Learning Outcomes

    After completing this module, you should be able to:
    🔗
    • Perform basic operations on vectors in \(\mathbb R^n\text{.}\)
      🔗
    • Interpret vectors geometrically in \(\mathbb R^2\) and \(\mathbb R^3\text{.}\)
      🔗
    • Convert between algebraic and geometric representations.
      🔗
    • Compute angles between vectors and test orthogonality.
      🔗
    • Compute orthogonal projections and rejections.
      🔗
    • Apply projections to distance problems.
      🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Vectors

    For more details on this material, see Open Linear Algebra (Preliminary Chapter).
    🔗

    Subsubsection Components and Length

    Let \(\mathbf v=\begin{bmatrix} v_1\\v_2\\\vdots\\v_n\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb R^n\text{.}\) The norm (or length) of \(\mathbf v\) is
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \|\mathbf v\| = \sqrt{v_1^2+v_2^2+\cdots+v_n^2}. \end{equation*}
    🔗

    Subsubsection Unit Vectors

    If \(\mathbf v\neq \mathbf 0\text{,}\) then the unit vector in the direction of \(\mathbf v\) is
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \widehat{\mathbf v} = \frac{\mathbf v}{\|\mathbf v\|}. \end{equation*}
    Example 12. Computing a Unit Vector.
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf v= \begin{bmatrix} 3\\4 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \|\mathbf v\| = 5 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \widehat{\mathbf v} = \begin{bmatrix} 3/5\\ 4/5 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Basic Vector Operations

    Subsubsection Vector Addition

    Vector addition is performed componentwise.
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf u+\mathbf v = \begin{bmatrix} u_1\\\vdots\\ u_n\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} v_1\\\vdots\\ v_n\end{bmatrix}= \begin{bmatrix} u_1+v_1\\\vdots\\ u_n+v_n\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Adding Vectors Geometrically

    Subsubsection Scalar Multiplication

    For a scalar \(\alpha\text{,}\)
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \alpha\mathbf u = \begin{bmatrix}\alpha u_1\\\vdots\\\alpha u_n\end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    Scaling changes a vector’s length. Negative scalars reverse direction.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Dot Product and Orthogonality

    Subsubsection Dot Product

    \begin{equation*} \mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v=\langle\mathbf u,\mathbf v\rangle = \sum_{i=1}^{n}u_iv_i. \end{equation*}
    Example 13. Dot Product Example.
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf u= \begin{bmatrix} 1\\2\\0\\1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf v= \begin{bmatrix} 0\\1\\-1\\1 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \langle\mathbf u,\mathbf v\rangle = 1\cdot0+2\cdot1+0\cdot(-1)+1\cdot1=3. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsubsection Angle Between Vectors

    Theorem 14. Angle Formula.
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v = \|\mathbf u\| \|\mathbf v\| \cos(\theta). \end{equation*}
    Hence,
    \begin{equation*} \theta = \arccos \left( \frac{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v} {\|\mathbf u\|\|\mathbf v\|} \right). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsubsection Orthogonality

    Vectors are orthogonal if and only if
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v=0. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Orthogonal Projection

    Subsubsection Projection Formula

    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf d}(\mathbf u) = \frac{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf d} {\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf d} \mathbf d. \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{oproj}_{\mathbf d}(\mathbf u) = \mathbf u - \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf d}(\mathbf u). \end{equation*}
    🔗

    Subsection Distance from a Point to a Line

    Consider a line
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} L= \{\mathbf A+t\mathbf d:t\in\mathbb R\}. \end{equation*}
    Let \(\mathbf{AB}=\mathbf B-\mathbf A\text{.}\) The shortest distance from \(B\) to the line is
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{dist}(B,L) = \left\| \mathbf{AB} - \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf d} (\mathbf{AB}) \right\|. \end{equation*}
    🔗

    Subsection Quick Reference

    \begin{equation*} \|\mathbf v\| = \sqrt{\sum_i v_i^2} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \widehat{\mathbf v} = \frac{\mathbf v}{\|\mathbf v\|} \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v = \sum_i u_i v_i \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \theta = \arccos \left( \frac{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v} {\|\mathbf u\|\|\mathbf v\|} \right) \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf d}(\mathbf u) = \frac{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf d} {\|\mathbf d\|^2}\mathbf d \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} \operatorname{oproj}_{\mathbf d}(\mathbf u) = \mathbf u-\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf d}(\mathbf u) \end{equation*}
